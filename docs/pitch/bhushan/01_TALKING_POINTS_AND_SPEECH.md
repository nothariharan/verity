## 1. Your Stage Identity & Tone

When Hariharan hands the spotlight to you, you are the **Brain of Verity**. 
* **Tone:** Calm, precise, and intellectually confident. Think of a senior AI researcher explaining why standard LLMs fail and how active learning solves the problem.
* **Goal:** Convince the judges that Verity is **not an open-ended ChatGPT prompt wrapper**. You must show that Verity has a structured reasoning engine that actively tests hypotheses.
* **Time Allocation:** Approximately **60 to 75 seconds** during the 4-minute presentation, plus taking the lead on any AI/ML questions during Q&A.

---

## 2. Your Exact Speaking Script (Word-for-Word)

When it's your turn to speak (usually right after the live demo moment where the case ring turns green):

### Step 1: The Transition & The Problem with Standard LLMs (15 Seconds)
> *"Thanks, Hariharan.  
> What you just saw on screen looks magical, but it’s actually driven by a very strict intelligence engine we call **The Mind**.  
> If you ask standard models like GPT-4 or Claude to conduct an interview, they immediately fail. They ask polite, generic, low-information questions like 'Can you tell me more about that?' That doesn't help a recruiter evaluate real engineering mastery."*

### Step 2: The Resume Extractor & The 3 Hypotheses (20 Seconds)
> *"Instead, our **Resume Extractor** ingests the candidate's resume against the Job Description and breaks every substantive technical claim into an active case file.  
> Every case holds three competing hypotheses:  
> **Owned** — they personally designed and operated it;  
> **Contributed** — they worked on a piece under someone else’s architecture; and  
> **Surface** — they know the vocabulary, but not the work.  
> Notice that we treat 'Contributed' as a completely respectable outcome for mid-level engineers—we don't force a fake pass/fail binary."*

### Step 3: MIT Battleship Active Learning Policy (20 Seconds)
> *"To decide what to ask next, we built our question policy on landmark 2026 active learning research from **MIT CSAIL**.  
> At every turn, Verity calculates the uncertainty across all cases and asks the single question that cuts the hypothesis space in half.  
> Specifically, it generates a **Pairwise Discriminator**: if a candidate is tied between 'Owned' and 'Contributed', Verity doesn't ask basic trivia—it asks about the architectural trade-offs and failure modes that only the true owner would know."*

### Step 4: The Bayesian Calibrator & "Forgetting is Not Lying" (15 Seconds)
> *"When the candidate answers, our **Bayesian Calibrator** updates the belief distribution based on concrete mechanism depth and personal agency.  
> And we enforce a critical ethical rule: **Forgetting is not lying.** If a candidate admits they don't remember a specific configuration flag from three years ago, our likelihood ratio is completely neutral. It moves nothing toward Surface and never triggers a contradiction.  
> That’s how Verity stays rigorous, objective, and deeply fair."*

*(Turn back to Hariharan or direct attention to the next slide).*

* **Point to Slide [01] (The Intelligence Mind):** Highlight the three icons:

  2. **MIT EIG Policy:** Highlight that the questions are targeted to resolve uncertainty, not picked from a static list.
  3. **Bayesian Calibrator:** Point to the 3-colored belief ring (Owned = Emerald, Contributed = Blue, Surface = Amber).

## 4. The 3 Things You Must Remember On Stage

1. **Never say:** *"The LLM decides what to ask."*  
   **Say:** *"Our Policy Engine selects the question based on Expected Information Gain."*
2. **Always emphasize "Contributed":** Judges love hearing that we don't treat non-architects as failures. Contributed is a great hire for feature engineering.
3. **Remember the catchphrase:** *"Quizzes test memorization. Verity investigates ownership."*
